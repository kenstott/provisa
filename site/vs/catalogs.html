Provisa / Works with / Provisa and data catalogs

Keep your catalog. Feed it what is actually enforced.

Catalogs are where people browse, search, classify and document data across every system, and Atlan, Collibra, Alation and DataHub all do this well. Provisa adds the running system behind that view: you declare sources, tables, relationships and governance in a model, and Provisa publishes it to your catalog.

What your catalog keeps doing

  • Finding and understanding data across all your systems, with descriptions and owners
  • Your business glossary: terms, definitions, synonyms and links to assets
  • Tagging and classification, and the places stewards already work

What Provisa adds

  • Masking, row-level security and column visibility enforced identically on every protocol and source type: SQL, GraphQL, Cypher, SPARQL, MCP and more
  • Column-level lineage derived from model definitions and published with its transforms
  • Descriptions that stay current, because every model change publishes
  • A typed ontology, with terms grounded directly to the tables and columns they mean

Use them together

Provisa publishes to your catalog automatically. Every governed table, column, domain, approved relationship, ontology term and column-level lineage edge flows to the target on each model change, typically within fifteen seconds. Your catalog does not need to crawl anything; it receives what Provisa knows.

Supported targets: OpenMetadata, DataHub, Collibra, Atlan (beta), Apache Atlas, Microsoft Purview, OpenLineage (Marquez and any OpenLineage consumer), Databricks Unity Catalog, Amazon DataZone, Google Dataplex, and Snowflake Horizon Catalog (data products). Configure one or several in Admin. Each gets the same snapshot, covering every domain you assign to it.

Governance signals travel with the publish. A masked column arrives in the catalog with the fact that it is masked and which roles the mask applies to. Rule bodies never leave Provisa, so the catalog does not expose your policy logic.

You can also take descriptions back. When a steward edits a table's description in the catalog, the Catalogs panel in Provisa lets them pull that edit into the model, table by table. Provisa never reads grants, row rules or masks from a catalog, because those belong in the model.

If you don't run a catalog yet

You may not need one. Every governed table and column has a description, steward, domain and business name in the model, with a typed ontology and column-level lineage alongside. If you add a catalog later, Provisa publishes to it.

If you only need a catalog

You want to document and classify data that already exists across many teams and systems. Governance is handled by each platform independently. The problem you are solving is visibility and discoverability, not uniformity of enforcement. A catalog covers that on its own.
